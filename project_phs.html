Product Health
Score.

There was no shared view of which features were working and which weren't. I built one.

Context

Roadmap decisions were made without a shared picture of feature performance.

Design intuition and product gut feeling were the main inputs to prioritisation. There was no consistent, repeatable way to look at a feature and say: this is working, this isn't, this needs investigation. Pendo was in place and generating data — but nobody was structuring it into something actionable.

I started this with a product analyst. No brief, no budget, no mandate. Just a problem that kept showing up in how decisions were made.

The gap

Usage data existed but wasn't structured. A feature could have high click counts and still be broken — dead clicks and rage clicks inflated the raw numbers. There was no quality filter.

The goal

A scoring model that could classify every feature in the product into actionable categories: what to protect, what to fix, what to investigate, and what to consider cutting.

The model

One score. Three inputs. Nine possible outcomes.

The Feature Health Score is a weighted composite pulled entirely from Pendo. The formula weights quality and depth heavily — a feature with a lot of clicks but a 60% failure rate should not look healthy.

40%
Success Rate
Quality gate. Clicks that actually complete an intended action, divided by total clicks. Below 80%: critical flag regardless of volume.
40%
Usage Depth
Relative intensity. Successful clicks normalised against the top-performing feature in the dataset, scaled 0–100. Not absolute volume.
20%
Adoption Rate
Reach. Unique users who touched this feature as a share of total app users. Matters, but shouldn't override quality.

Before a feature gets scored, it passes through a quality gate: if its success rate is below 80%, it's automatically flagged as a critical issue regardless of any other metric. Volume means nothing if the failure rate is high.

Why relative, not absolute. Normalising against the highest-performing feature in the dataset — not against some external benchmark — means the model works at any product scale. A feature with 3,200 successful clicks and a feature with 140 are both meaningful in context.

What the score produces

Each feature lands in one of nine categories. The categories are determined by the quality gate first, then a 3×3 matrix of depth vs reach. The intent is to make the next action obvious.

🏆
Core Asset
High depth, high reach. Protect. These drive primary product value.
🚨
Critical Quality Issue
Success rate below 80%. Fix before anything else.
💎
Specialized Utility
High depth, low reach. Valuable to a specific segment. Retain.
✅
Balanced & Stable
Healthy middle tier. Monitor but no urgent action.
📉
Low Engagement
Low depth and low reach. Investigate before the next cycle.
🔍
Product Audit
Minimal footprint. Evaluate for sunsetting or repositioning.
⚖️
Emerging / Niche
Medium depth, low reach. Watch for growth signals.
💤
Inactive
Zero interaction in the period. Review if the feature is even reachable.

Notes

First cycle data. 74 features scored. Upload Image flagged immediately — 1,280 dead clicks on a 2,370 total.

The Upload Image finding was the first real output. A 45% success rate on a feature 233 users had touched in the period. The 1,280 dead clicks pointed to a UI element users were repeatedly clicking that wasn't responding. That went straight into the next sprint.

How it was built

From a spreadsheet to a repeatable process.

The first version was just a working formula and a manual process. The point was to validate whether the output was useful before designing anything around it. Once the PM and analyst confirmed the categories matched their intuition and added signal they didn't have, the process got documented.

01
Formula design
Defined what a "healthy feature" meant in terms of measurable signals. Tested weight combinations against known features to validate the output made sense.
02
First cycle
Ran the model against live Pendo data. 74 features, one sheet, one pass. Identified two critical quality failures and four features that had never registered a single interaction.
03
Playbook
Documented the full setup so the analyst can run subsequent cycles without me. Includes the formula logic, the Pendo export steps, and the anchor configuration.

On keeping it in Excel. A custom dashboard would have looked better. But the analyst already works in Excel, the PM reads Excel, and the model needs to be maintainable by people who aren't me. The right tool for this was the one that would actually be used quarterly.

Impact

What the first cycle surfaced.

The value wasn't just the score. It was having a shared vocabulary. Core Asset, Critical Issue, Product Audit — the PM and analyst started using those terms in sprint planning. That's when the process became something, not just a spreadsheet.

01
Quarterly cadence
The model runs at the start of each quarter. Results feed directly into the roadmap review. The comparison to the previous cycle shows trend direction, not just a snapshot.
02
Second product
A second dataset was run against the same model for a different product. Same formula, different benchmark anchors. The framework is portable across the portfolio.
03
Open questions
How to weight new features with low data yet. Whether to add a qualitative layer for features known to serve a small but high-value user segment. Still being worked out.
